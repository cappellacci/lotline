### LB-NNN · 2026-10-20 · Build: local demand index (shift-share employment, C04)
- **Question:** can we give every county, place and township in MN, NC, OH and TX a local labor-demand measure built only from its pre-period industry mix, so WS-ENGINE can use it as the C04 control and modifier (D-023: it replaces ACS household growth when available)?
- **What I did:**
  - New adapter `src/lotline/adapters/national/cbp.py`: Census County Business Patterns paid employment by 2-digit NAICS sector, county and U.S., 2010–2023, one request per state-year plus one national request per year (70 files) through the IO layer. Cells withheld through 2016 (EMP 0 with a range flag a–m) are filled inside their published range at `suppressed_cell_position` and marked imputed; from 2017 CBP adds noise instead, so nothing is imputed.
  - New build step `src/lotline/build/demand.py` (CLI source `demand`): `demand_bartik` = Σ sectors (2010–2012 mean share × national log growth since 2012); `demand_bartik_loo` leaves the county's own jobs out of the national totals; places are housing-unit-weighted means of their counties (`geoparts.county_shares`), and townships take their county's value. `demand_imputed_share` reports how much of the mix rests on filled cells.
  - Parameters in `config/params/demand.yaml` (share window, base year, fill position, each with a source). Schemas 1.8 adds the three optional `market_geo_year` columns (issue #186, `contract-change`).
- **Inputs:** Census CBP API (public domain; CENSUS_API_KEY), 2020 Census place-by-county housing-unit splits, processed `jurisdictions`.
- **Outputs:** `src/lotline/adapters/national/cbp.py`, `src/lotline/build/demand.py`, `config/params/demand.yaml`, `tests/core/test_demand.py`, `docs/schemas.md`, `docs/data_provenance.md` (CBP row and section), `docs/model_and_assumptions.md` (parameter table); data in `processed/<st>/market_geo_year/demand.parquet` (outside the repo).
- **Findings:**
  - Coverage, 2010–2023 with no missing values: MN 87 counties, 759 places, 218 townships; NC 100 counties, 222 places; OH 88 counties, 566 places, 297 townships; TX 253 counties, 947 places.
  - The index varies little across counties: 2023 standard deviation 0.015–0.020 log points in MN, NC and OH and 0.041 in TX, around a mean of about 0.16. At the 2-digit sector level most counties' mixes track the nation. Expect it to identify little on its own; it still fixes the cycle (e.g. the 2021 dip) consistently across places.
  - Leave-one-out barely matters (largest gap 0.006, Texas's biggest counties). Suppression matters more in rural Texas: 46 of 253 TX counties have over half their pre-period mix imputed (MN 8, NC 3, OH 3).
- **Decisions:** proposed for HANDOFF: schemas 1.8 (issue #186); the demand index uses 2-digit NAICS, a 2010–2012 share window and a 2012 base year. Places that adopted a reform before 2013 have post-adoption shares (noted in the params file).
- **AI:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5) wrote the adapter, build step, parameters and tests. It checked the API's behaviour with a few small probe requests (flags present for 2010–2016, absent for 2017+; 2023 latest vintage). The tests include a hand-worked two-industry example (0.7·ln 1.2 + 0.3·ln 0.8 = 0.0607), a leave-one-out case on a synthetic two-county nation, flag-range filling at positions 0, 0.5 and 1, and place weighting. Full suite: 828 passed. Ruff, compliance (0 FAIL) and the params-table check pass. The real-data numbers above were sanity-checked: Harris and Travis counties' paths are plausible, with a 2021 COVID dip.
- **Open / next:** WS-ENGINE wires `demand_bartik` into D-024/D-025 as C04. If more variation is needed, a 3-digit NAICS version is the next step, at the cost of more suppressed cells. Rerun with `suppressed_cell_position` at 0 and 1 as a sensitivity check.
