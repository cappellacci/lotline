- **Question:** On North Carolina's ADU results chart, the no-reform forecast for 2027–2031 (midpoint ~650 a year) is more than twice the permits counted in 2023–2025 (~290 a year). Why, and what should a judge be told?

- **Findings:**
  - The history bars count only the places whose permit records flag ADUs, home to about 46% of NC's people (2023/24/25: 288/278/290; largest in 2025 Raleigh 61, Wilmington 59, Charlotte 45, Durham 44, Asheville 38). The forecast covers every place. The no-reform range is P10 ~385 to P90 ~1,200, so even P10 is above every year counted.
  - My first read was that coverage drove the gap and the drift was secondary. The ADU owner session then measured the split with an engine run on current main (origin 2025, 1,000 draws, average over 2027–31). That measurement corrects this read: **the per-place drift is the main cause.** The 23 places with their own counts reach P50 453 (269–807), even though their own medians add up to 306, close to their k-adjusted pace of 298. The 427 estimated places add P50 136 (56–487, mean 258). With the drift off (σ = 0), the counted places are P50 303 and the state total P50 441. So the drift exp(σz), σ 1.025, adds ~150 a year to the counted places and ~210 to the total. Coverage adds ~140, and the undercount k is minor. My guess of ~360 from estimated places was too high.
  - The counted-place total differs: 23 in the engine, 21 in the chart's `results_observed`. The owner session reconciled it. All 23 places have permit sources that flag ADUs, so each gets its own rate. At least six of them (Wrightsville Beach, Kure Beach, Fairview, Stallings, Concord, Chapel Hill) recorded 0 ADUs a year over the observed window, so the count depends on how zero-count places are treated. This doesn't change the gap. The owner session is checking separately whether Concord's and Chapel Hill's zeros are real.
  - The page already says the bars "understate the state" in small print, but the chart draws a partial count and a statewide forecast on one scale, which invites the question.
