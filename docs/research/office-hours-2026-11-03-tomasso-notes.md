# Office hours with Amy Tomasso (mentor), 2026-11-03

First mentor session. Ben walked through the approach (ADU + Missing Middle + Fees; four development states MN/NC/TX/OH; per-1,000-eligible-parcel rate; causal diagram; backtesting). Transcript kept outside the repo. Mentor office hours run every Tuesday to the end of the challenge; questions in between go in the Teams chat.

## What Amy said (feedback, in order)

1. **Metric.** Per-1,000 is unusual for her; she asked twice whether the baseline (no-reform) scenario is the comparison. Her instinct is a comparative-cost headline (Portland's Residential Infill Project: new missing-middle homes reported ~$300k cheaper than new detached houses) because it reads as affordability. Unit counts are fine but "1,000 might be too big for this size": a rate of ~2 per 1,000 reads as nothing to a legislator. She wants the number at least in the hundreds, or expressed as a share of the state's quantified housing need / growth projection ("what percentage of that would 100 ADUs be, even 5 in a rural state").
2. **Generalizability.** Sold on the four-state backtest and confounder-adjustment argument ("good job there"). The weakness is argumentation around small effects, not the method.
3. **Property-rights framing.** She has heard it (New Hampshire); many legislators won't be moved by "a lot of effort just to let people do what they want". Keep it as a secondary argument.
4. **Other states.** Look at Washington and Oregon (early missing-middle leaders) and Vermont (statewide ADU legalization; she heard the day before that the market there is now supporting ADU construction). Repeated at the close: "look at some other states".
5. **Ohio caveat.** The housing problem there has a different flavor (Cleveland: plentiful, cheap old stock where renovation costs exceed value). Oregon's missing-middle uptake rides on Oregon being a desirable market. Don't read Ohio's low uptake as a reform failure, and don't read Oregon's as transferable without the demand context.
6. **Fee baseline.** Our observed fees (roughly $20k on the high end, incl. permits, connections, inspections) are below the Handbook's $50k. Her advice: be truthful, state it as a caveat ("this assumption is high for the states I modeled; here is what I found"), note California may be what $50k is based on (Utah is also lower). Selling angle: impact fees are being debated as a revenue source in lieu of property taxes in some cities; the result doubles as "here is why not to raise them".
7. **Fee mechanism.** Don't model prices (developers still take market rate). Model the supply side: does the fee cut make an extra unit pencil? Compare pro formas for a single-family house, a duplex and a fourplex; per-unit utility connection fees are "the biggest problem of all for missing middle and often why it doesn't pencil". Use it as a density argument. (Timeline reduction came to mind but she set it aside.)
8. **ADU rents.** She asked whether ADU legalization lowered rents anywhere; Ben said the share of inventory is too small to see it. Fine, but it should appear as a stated limitation.
