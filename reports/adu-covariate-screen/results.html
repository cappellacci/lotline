Renters and older homes: two factors that help explain where ADUs get built

We refitted the ADU model once for each place factor it doesn't use, and asked whether the factor explains why places with the same rules build so differently. Two factors pass the bar set before the run, both narrowly. Nothing in the model has changed.

Finding 1

Places with more renters build more ADUs per eligible lot

A place about 15 points more owner-occupied builds roughly half as many ADUs per eligible lot, with the same rules and market. Owner share most likely stands in for how urban a place is and how much rental demand an extra unit meets. It is the only factor that also works across states: Minnesota's places are the most owner-occupied, the same direction as the model's Minnesota over-prediction.

Within-state version: one rate per state, so each factor is compared only between places in the same state. Blue marks the two candidates.

Finding 2

Older housing helps within a state, but not between states

A place with about 17 points more homes built before 1960 builds about 1.8 times as many ADUs per eligible lot. That fits the garages, alleys and lot layouts of older neighbourhoods. In the pooled version, which can also compare states, its effect fades. Owner share grows stronger there instead.

Pooled version: one rate for all places.

Finding 3

Each candidate explains a modest slice of the gap between places

The place-effect variance α measures how much places differ that the model can't explain. Owner share cuts it by 16% and housing age by 11%. The bar was 10%, marked by the dashed line. New-house building, lot size, place size and home value each move it by 4% or less.
