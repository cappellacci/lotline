Do places that newly allow ADUs build less at first?

The gut check found that under the Handbook reform, cities that allow no ADUs today (Dallas, Fort Worth, Cleveland, now Akron) get the full rate of cities with established ADU programs. D-099 asked whether the 28 counted places that first allowed ADUs after 2016 built less in their first five years than similar places that already allowed them.

Verdict: no change to the model. In the fit they do build somewhat less, about 75% of the mature rate, but that doesn't predict any better for a place left out (log score -0.11, 2 SE 1.86). D-099's rule needs both, so ν stays 1.

How strongly the data favour a discount

The profile peaks at 0.75 and drops off on both sides. At 1.0 (no discount) it is 5.5 log-likelihood units below the peak: a real but small preference, carried mostly by Minnesota suburbs that counted few or none.

What this leaves

  • No first-years discount. Places that newly allow ADUs build only a little less at first, and that doesn't carry over to a held-out place.
  • The Dallas gap was mostly the average, not the model. After this run we found the gut check had compared Dallas's average over draws (2.3 a year per 1,000 lots) with other cities' actual rates. Its median is 0.35 (P10–P90 0.01–5.1), below what Austin (0.73) and Portland (1.2) built. A single city's average is pulled far above its median by its own place luck. The gut-check page is corrected.
  • One coding question remains: since 2018 Dallas allows rentable ADUs by special exception or overlay, coded as "no ADUs". Recoding it as a restricted rule would scale Dallas's own tiny count instead, which would almost erase its gain.
