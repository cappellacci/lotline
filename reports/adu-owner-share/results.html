Finding 1

Every held-out state moves toward its observed count

We left each state out, refitted on the other four, and predicted the state's 2021–2024 ADUs from its 20 largest places' own rules (tier 1). Minnesota's over-prediction shrinks from ×3.4 to ×2.1. North Carolina, Texas and Oregon's under-predictions shrink by roughly a third to a half. North Carolina gets inside ×2 at tier 1. Texas and Oregon do not.

Ohio cannot be scored: its one fit place, Columbus, is its tier-2 city. Every range still holds the observed total. D-075 counts a state as useful when the range holds the total and the median is within ×2.

Finding 2

New places: renter-heavy cities get closer, but College Station overshoots

Test A predicts each place added since D-053 from a fit without it. The renter-heavy North Carolina and Oregon cities move closer: Durham from ×4.7 to ×2.5, Wilmington from ×5.0 to ×2.2, Charlotte to ×1.0. North Carolina now passes test A. College Station, the most renter-heavy place in the fit (z −2.1), swings from right on to a 4× over-prediction, so Texas no longer passes. The count of tests passed is unchanged: 1 each.

Form AForm A′

Test B (the Wake and Franklin County towns' roll counts) still fails for both forms. A′ narrows the over-prediction (Wake: median 69 against 5 counted, down from 83), but not enough.

Finding 3

The effect is steady, though weaker than the screen suggested

β_o keeps its sign whichever state is left out, and with the 2016–20 vintage. Its interval crosses zero only without Minnesota, and in the within-state version (−0.37, −0.77 to +0.03). It is smaller than D-083's −0.81. Most of the difference is the six Minnesota townships, which the screen lacked and which are about 95% owner-occupied. Adding pre-1960 housing on top changes nothing (p 0.45).
