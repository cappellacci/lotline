Does a steadier baseline fix Texas?

D-054 missed Texas because Lubbock's last three pre-reform years predicted far more building than came. D-058 flags places whose pre-reform pace fails a Poisson dispersion test and gives them a six-year baseline. It also floors zero baselines, so "no effect" can be scored. D-054's leave-one-state-out backtest was then run again. Texas is still missed, and by the pre-registered rule the D-055 values stay.

Finding 1 · Texas

Lubbock's whole pre-reform period was high, so no pre-reform baseline can catch the fall

The test flagged Lubbock, and its baseline widened to six years. Its baseline falls from 115 to 90 buildings a year, so it still expects 160 over 2024–25, against 25 permitted, because the pace was high in every pre-reform year. Every specification and both benchmarks miss the Texas total. Lubbock's log score improves by 26 units, but not enough to bring Texas inside any range. What changed in Lubbock after 2023 needs checking in the data (permit system, BPS reporting, a local pause), not in the model.

Finding 2 · The fitted rate

The data can't tell a fast, small effect from a slow, larger one

The likelihood has two peaks. At one, the effect arrives in year 1 and θ is about 0.12 per 1,000. At the other, it builds slowly (half-way at 6 years, the grid's end) toward a "mature" θ near 1.3. Dropping one place that built nothing, or adding the zero floor, moves a fit from one peak to the other. The dots below are each fit's θ; colour shows which peak it landed on.

Finding 3 · What the two peaks mean over five years

The slow peak's bigger θ means fewer homes inside the scenario window

The scenario reports five years. The realized hazard each year is θ times the ramp. On the slow peak, θ 1.28 reaches only 0.15 per 1,000 by year 5 and averages 0.04 over years 1–5. The adopted D-055 values average 0.10. So the main fit's headline θ is ten times larger, yet it implies less building over five years. A range for the scenario should be stated on this five-year rate.

Finding 4 · Signal against no effect

With the −∞ gap closed, the gain over no effect is under 2 SE

The zero floor makes no effect's log score finite everywhere, so item 2 can now be scored. The main model gains 10.8 log units over no effect on the 13 fit places (paired SE 8.8); D-054's model with the floor gains 13.8 (9.4). Both fall short of the 2-SE bar. North Carolina's towns are now inside every range, no effect included. Almost all of the gain is Wake Forest (+7.7) and Burlington (+4.5).

Outcome by the rule · and what Ben could do next

D-055's values stay. Nothing in config changes.

  • Rule items: 1, transfer: not met (Texas missed). 2, signal: not met (+10.8 against a bar of 17.6). 3, not worse than (b′): met. So mm_hazard.yaml keeps θ 0.12, half-way 0.5, and Charlotte freezes on those.
  • Check Lubbock's data: did something outside the reform cut its 2024–25 permits (a permit-system change, a BPS imputation, a moratorium)? If so, it should be coded or dropped under a new entry, not modelled.
  • Carry the ramp problem into the scenario: state the missing-middle range on the five-year realized rate (about 0.04–0.10 per 1,000 a year across the two peaks), or hold the ramp at an outside value (1.5–5 years, D-038) and refit θ alone. Either is a new entry.
  • Read the signal plainly: outside two North Carolina towns, the four states' 2–4 unit permits don't yet show a reform effect that beats each place's own trend at 2 SE.
