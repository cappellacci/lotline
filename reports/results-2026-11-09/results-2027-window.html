Five states, 2027–2031: the first run on the new window

The first results runs since the window moved to 2027–2031 (D-061), the baseline began counting rules in force by the end of 2027 (D-064) and housing need began covering mid-2026 to mid-2031. Each state was run four ways, 1,000 draws each, so every change can be seen on its own: A the old setup (2026–2030, end-2025 rules, old need); B the new window with end-2025 rules; C the new window with end-2027 rules for everything; D end-2027 rules for each place's own rules and the end-2025 mix for places that borrow (D-067, the current official bundles). All figures are net homes added statewide over five years, after completion and displacement.

Finding 2 · The window

Moving the window to 2027–2031 changes the labels, not the homes

Runs A and B give the same five-year homes in every state and scenario, to the home. The added homes are measured against the baseline, and the scenarios start their clock at the window's first year either way. The one real change is in ADUs: the 2026 lead year removes a few parcels from the pool before the reform starts, about 0.03% of it, which rounds away. What does move is the housing-need denominator, and with it the share of need.

Finding 4 · Where the change comes from

Most of the drop comes from places that borrow their rules, not from the places that changed

A place with no coded rule borrows the rule mix of coded places its size (D-022 for ADUs, the dose pool for missing middle). When a few places gain rules in 2026–27, every borrower moves with them. Below, each change in the scenario's opening (ADUs: eligible parcels × (1 − E[c]); missing middle: newly legal parcels) is split between places with their own coded rule and places that borrow.

places with their own ruleplaces that borrow
  • NC ADUs: the 19 S.L. 2026-59 cities already allowed ADUs, but their local rules were read as covering 75% of eligible lots. The state law covers every residential lot, so E[c] goes from 0.75 to 1.0 in Fayetteville, Concord, Gastonia, Asheville, Huntersville and others. That is the direct third. The other two thirds comes from borrowers, led by Wake County's unincorporated area.
  • TX ADUs: McAllen, League City and Lewisville gain coded rules, and Killeen moves from "no ADUs" to allowed. The large county remainders (Harris, Montgomery, Bexar, Fort Bend) borrow, and Harris alone accounts for about a tenth of the change.
  • Missing middle, MN and NC: only 8 of Minnesota's 1,051 places and 22 of North Carolina's 453 have a coded rule. Duluth's 2026 rule (dose 0 → 0.75) removes 22,900 legal parcels directly, and the borrowers in its size band about 144,000 more.

Decided · Ben, 2026-11-09: end-2025 mix (D-067)

Should places that borrow their rules see 2026–27 rules, or the end-2025 mix?

D-064 moved one date for everything: a place's own rules and the coded mix that uncoded places borrow. The run shows the second part dominates, and it rests on few coded places (8 in Minnesota for missing middle).

  1. Keep as is. Borrowers see the 2027 mix. Consistent: the mix is meant to describe current rules, and these are current. But a handful of newly coded places swing hundreds of thousands of parcels, and NC's state law covers only its 19 named cities, not the unincorporated areas that now borrow from them.
  2. Borrow from the end-2025 mix (recommended). Each place's own rules use the D-064 date; the mix uncoded places borrow stays at the data origin, which is also when the rules were coded and checked. D-064's effect then sits on the places whose law actually changed. Estimated from the split above (not a run): NC ADUs about 112 instead of 79 (B was 126), and missing middle in MN close to B's 462.

Ben chose the end-2025 mix. D-067 was written and pushed (bf563eb) before the code change (96a5de2) and the run.
