Lotline · Missing middle · D-092 runs 9–11, refit with lumpy years · 15 Nov 2026

Honest ranges now hold up, and they are too wide to show the reform's effect

With each year's count allowed to be lumpy (φ = 13.6, fitted on places the model never uses), the missing-middle model's ranges are calibrated: 84% of places land inside P10–P90, against 44% before. Leave one state out and the parcel-roll check now pass. Test C misses on one rule, Minnesota's three-place total. But the same honesty swamps the reform signal: the model beats "no effect" by only 5.0 (SE 3.4), and the placebo still fails.

Finding 2

The ranges now reach the observed totals, because they are much wider

Run 5, Poisson baselineRun 11, lumpy years

Before, Ohio's and Texas's no-reform totals fell outside narrow ranges. Now they fall inside, as does Oregon left out. Oregon's range runs to nearly 10,000 against 703 built, which is too wide to guide a bill.

Finding 3

The reform effect is no longer clear

Once a place's no-reform path can swing the way real places do, the cap rises in this data add little that the model can tell apart from noise. Moving every event three years early gives a five-year rate of 0.00045, more than the real dates' 0.00027. Oregon and the other four states agree much more closely (θ 1.68 with Oregon, 1.08 without), so the 20× gap in run 4 was mostly the over-sure baseline.

For Ben

Next: place types, then count projects instead of buildings

Place types (core city, suburb, town, as in ADU D-088) are already planned. They can explain part of the year-to-year spread and narrow every place's range without new data. The deeper fix is the unit: buildings arrive in projects, and run 1 found about 11 permit sources with parcel IDs. Counting projects would make the noise model match the mechanism. Nothing goes to config until you choose; the tool still runs D-059's θ = 0.12.
