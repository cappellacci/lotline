Lotline · D-090 ADU within-state forecast · 14 Nov 2026

A state's own ADU history does not forecast its next five years inside the range

We fitted each state on its own 2016–2019 permits and forecast 2021–2025 under the rules actually in force. Form A with each place's own history (F1) was useful in 1 of 4 states; the pre-set bar was 3. It did no better than carrying each place's old rate forward, and housing age did not help. Every forecaster's range was far too narrow.

What the pre-set rule says. All three rules read against the model:

  1. Own history is not enough. F1 is useful only in North Carolina. So, as D-090 fixed before the run, the tool shows the ranges and says plainly that ADU uptake cannot yet be forecast within ×2, even with local history.
  2. The mechanism does not beat persistence. F1 − F0 is -5.5 against a bar of 204. The spread is huge because a few cities swing it both ways (finding 3).
  3. Housing age does not replace F1. F2 − F1 is -41.9 (bar 88), useful in 0 states.

Accept this outcome? If yes, the next question is the range, not the median (finding 2).

Finding 2

Finding 3

Where rules changed, the mechanism was right in North Carolina and wrong in Minneapolis and Bend

Places with at least 50 ADUs in the window (· = a coded rule change in 2021–2025). In Wilmington and Charlotte, F1 priced the rule change and persistence missed by ×3 to ×6. In Minneapolis, F1 expected the 2021 change to raise output; output fell. Bend had no change in the window, but its stronger rule took effect partway through the fit years, so F1 projected the full-rule rate, about 3 times what came.

Finding 5

The fit-year trends all pointed down, and NC then rose

A log-linear trend fitted per state on 2016–2019 falls in every state. It helps Oregon and Minnesota, where counts kept falling, and doubles the miss in North Carolina, where counts surged after 2020. D-090 kept it as a sensitivity for this reason.
