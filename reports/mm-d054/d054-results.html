Testing the missing-middle rate on state totals

Each state's new 2–4 unit buildings were predicted from a fit to the other three states, then compared with what BPS recorded. The main model is D-038's, refitted on today's coding, so only building types each reform newly allows are counted. It is compared with D-042, with the values now in mm_hazard.yaml, and with two benchmarks. The pre-registered reading rule says the adopted values stay. The reason is a Texas baseline failure that every model shares.

Finding 1 · The fitted rate

On today's coding the rate is about a ninth of the adopted one

θ is the yearly chance, per 1,000 eligible single-family lots, of a new 2–4 unit building once a full rule has matured. The four-state refit gives 0.12; the adopted D-038 value is 1.08. It was fitted before Austin and Houston's earlier duplex rules were coded. Dropping one state at a time keeps θ between 0.01 and 0.24, and North Carolina carries most of it.

Finding 2 · Leave one state out

North Carolina passes, Texas fails for every model

The dot is what was built; the bar is P10–P90. North Carolina's six towns land inside the main model's range with the median close. No effect and D-042 both miss them. Texas is missed by every model and both benchmarks. Minnesota and Ohio have one place each, with one year and no buildings, so they show nothing.

Finding 3 · Why Texas misses · post-hoc diagnostic

Lubbock's pre-reform pace stopped, and every model assumed it would continue

Each model adds the reform's effect on top of a place's own pre-reform pace. Lubbock built about 115 two-to-four unit buildings a year before its 2024 rule and 25 in the two years after. That one baseline puts 191 expected buildings into the Texas total before any reform effect. Without Lubbock, Texas built 24. That is inside the main model's range and above anything no effect allows; Houston's 21 three-to-four unit buildings are most of it.

Finding 4 · The test places

Minneapolis and Durham built twice what their own trends implied

Minneapolis, Durham city and Durham County were never in any fit. Adding them to their state's total, the main model holds Minnesota but falls short in North Carolina: Durham city built 100 against a median of 41. The wide adopted range and the outside-evidence rate hold both. No effect misses both, so these places show a real reform response that the refit's low θ understates.

Finding 5 · Held-out log score by place

Against no effect, the gain is two places

Each bar is how much more probable the main model made a place's observed years than no effect did, in log units. Wake Forest built 9 buildings on a zero baseline, which no effect calls impossible. That makes the pre-registered comparison infinite, so its 2-SE rule can't be evaluated as written. Leaving Wake Forest out (post-hoc), the main model gains 9.8 with a paired SE of 11.1, almost all from Houston and Burlington. D-042 does worse than the main model (−62.9, SE 51.8).

Finding 6 · Spread between states (report only)

Four states can't measure how much states differ

τ is the log-SD of a state's multiplier on θ. Its profile falls 0.8 log-likelihood units from 0 to 2.5, far less than the 1.35 needed to bound it. So τ is not estimable. Drawn from the flat profile, a new state's multiplier would run ×0.23–×4.2. Nothing goes to config.
