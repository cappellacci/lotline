Lotline · D-085 ADU time trend · 13 Nov 2026

A yearly trend fits the past ADU counts far better, but forecasts 2021–25 no better, so the model keeps no trend

D-085 asked two things: does uptake drift by calendar year, and can the model forecast the places it has seen? We added one shared yearly trend, and ran a new test: fit on 2016–20, forecast 2021–25. The trend explains a lot in sample, mostly Oregon's boom and fall, but it does not forecast better. A plain carry-forward of each place's own early rate does as well as any model form.

  2. Form A′ + trend: test D gain 0.7 is not above 2 SE (15.3); fewer of tests A-B passed (0 vs 1).

Nothing goes to config. The open call is still D-084 (adopt Form A′ or keep Form A); this result does not change it.

Finding 1

The trend is mostly Oregon's boom and fall

Fitted on every place, uptake relative to the model falls about 12% a year. It stays near that when Minnesota, North Carolina, Ohio or Texas is left out. Without Oregon it shrinks to about 4% a year, and the likelihood gain drops from 664 to 26. Oregon's large cities issued ADU permits at a peak in the late 2010s and fewer since. A shared trend mostly encodes that one boom.

A rule-age term in place of the calendar trend (outlook only) is weaker: δ -0.051 a year of rule age, likelihood ratio 23 against Form A′.

Finding 2

Forecasting 2021–25 from 2016–20: no form is reliable, and the simplest forecast does as well

This is the scenario's own job: forecast places the model has already seen. Every form misses Minnesota badly, and only North Carolina lands within ×2 and inside the range. Holding the trend flat helps Texas and Oregon but not enough to clear the bar. Carrying each place's own 2016–20 rate forward scores -146.2, against Form A's -151.0.

Finding 3

Minneapolis: the model predicts a huge jump at the 2021 reform, and none came

Fitted on 2016–20 alone, the share an owner-occupancy rule allows comes out at 0.1 (it is 1.0 on all years). So when Minneapolis dropped its owner-occupancy rule in 2021, the model expected uptake to rise about tenfold. It forecast 1,583 ADU permits for 2021–25; 72 were counted, fewer than the carry-forward's 145. This is most of Minnesota's miss. It is also the question D-086 asks: how much a rule change moves uptake. Here the model's step is far too big, the opposite of Raleigh's case.
