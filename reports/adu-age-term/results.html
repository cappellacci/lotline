Finding 2

Housing age adds nothing held-out on top of owner share

A″ against A′ directly: +0.15 in held-out log score, against 2 SE of about 3.6. In-sample, age's likelihood-ratio statistic over A′ is 3.3 on one degree of freedom. The two terms are correlated (r -0.39 across places), and owner share takes most of what age offers. Alone, age does about a third as well (LR 5.5 against A, compared with 14.3 for owner share).

At tier 1, Minnesota's over-prediction shrinks most under A′. A″ gives back part of that gain, likely because Minnesota's suburbs are both owner-heavy and newer.

Finding 4

Between states it is owner share; within states it is housing age

In the pooled A″, both β's keep their sign whichever state is left out. Owner share's interval stays clear of zero every time. Age's crosses zero without North Carolina. With one θ per state, the picture flips: owner share falls to -0.09 (SE 0.28), and age rises to +0.87 (SE 0.26). Much of owner share's pooled pull is the difference between states. Age's is the difference between places inside a state. The joint draws carry their correlation (r 0.22).
