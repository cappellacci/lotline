Lotline · D-093: the engine reads D-091's adopted outcome · 15 Nov 2026

Using each place's own ADU counts barely moves the homes a reform adds, and lowers today's baseline

Where a place has its own ADU counts, the engine now takes its no-reform ADUs from those counts, times a drift of ×2.8 either way at one SD, and scales them by the model's with-reform ÷ no-reform ratio for the Handbook bill. That is the outcome Ben adopted after D-090 and D-091. Elsewhere the A + place-types model runs as before. Additional homes at the median move by 10% or less in every state. The no-reform baseline falls in Minnesota, North Carolina and Ohio, where the model put places with counts above what they actually build, and its top end roughly halves there.

Finding 1

Additional homes from the Handbook ADU reform barely change

The reform's effect still comes from the model's ratio, so the added homes stay close to before. Texas and Ohio rise most, by about 9–10% at the median. Texas's P90 rises more, because its six places with counts (Austin among them) hold a sixth of its lots and their drift is wide.

Before: A + place types, observed rate as a floorNow: own counts with drift where a place has them
Finding 2

ADUs under today's rules fall, and their range narrows

Before, a place with counts got the larger of the model's rate and its observed rate. On the whole the model ran higher than those places build, so its spread set the top of the range. Now the place's own rate sets it. Texas barely moves at the median, and Oregon rises a little: their places with counts build about as many as, or more than, the model says.

Finding 3

Which places changed

Only places with observed ADU counts change; the rest give identical numbers both ways (same seeds). They are few in Ohio and Texas but hold 16–48% of eligible lots, because they include the big cities.

What it cannot say

Three limits

  • The bill's ratio is unchecked. D-090 found the model within ×2 in 1 of 4 states. The ratio may be better than the level, but no held-out test shows it. The app should label a bill's effect "not checked within ×2".
  • Independent drift averages out in state totals. Each place draws its own drift, median 1, mean 1.69. Summed over many places, the state total drifts toward the mean, and its range narrows. D-091 found one drift shared by a state covered state totals better; that is a sensitivity, not what runs here.
  • The drift was learned on a different gap. σ 1.025 comes from forecasting 2021–25 from four years, 2016–19. The engine reads three years, 2023–25, and forecasts 2027–31.
