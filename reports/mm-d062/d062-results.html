The five-year missing-middle rate doesn't depend on the ramp

D-062 asked whether the scenario's range was too wide at the top. The fitted rate θ and the ramp's speed trade off, and the scenario holds the ramp at its fastest. The run fitted the quantity the scenario reports instead: the mean yearly rate over the five scenario years, r5 = θ × mean ramp share. It used two fits on the fit-set places only. The premise turned out to be wrong, and no config change is needed.

Finding 1 · Ramp speed

Slow or fast ramp, the five years come out at about 0.1 buildings per 1,000 lots a year

For each ramp half-way point from 0.5 to 6 years, the best θ (orange) rises 20-fold, from 0.12 to 2.5 per 1,000. But θ times the ramp's five-year mean share (green) stays between 0.08 and 0.11. The data pin down the five-year rate and leave the split between θ and ramp speed open. That split doesn't matter for a five-year total. Bars show each fit's 90% interval with the ramp held. Under the adopted fit, ramps of 0.5–1 year carry 97% of the likelihood.

Finding 2 · The range

θ's wide top end comes from the fast ramp itself, so the range in config is already right

D-062 assumed θ's upper bound (1.006 per 1,000) belonged to a slow ramp. It doesn't: with the ramp held at 0.5, θ's own interval is 0.033–1.006. The width comes from how much places differ (α ≈ 8), not from the ridge. So the five-year range the scenario draws now (0.027–0.85) is exactly the r5 profile interval. Without Lubbock it is a little narrower at the top (0.030–0.74). Drawing ramp speeds jointly by their likelihood gives a similar centre with a thinner top (P95 0.56).

Finding 3 · The profile

One peak in r5, with a long tail upward

The likelihood-ratio statistic of r5, maximized over ramp speed and place spread. Values under the dashed line (2.71) are inside the 90% interval. Both fits peak near 0.10–0.11. The curve is flatter to the right: high rates stay plausible because a few places could be very responsive.

For Ben to decide

Keep the config as it is. D-062's proposed new key isn't needed.

  • No change to mm_hazard.yaml (suggested). Drawing θ from its interval with the ramp at 0.5 already gives the right five-year range. The mm_five_year_hazard key D-062 proposed would duplicate it.
  • V5 (Nov 17) can freeze on the current values. The worry D-062 raised for V5 doesn't apply.
  • D-063's scenario uses θ × ramp(half 0.5), as V9 does. The ridge is reported as a finding: it changes when the homes come, not how many come in five years.
  • Caveat: under D-058's stability rule (d), the fit sat at the slow ramp with r5 ≈ 0.04. That specification wasn't adopted, and this run doesn't revisit it.
