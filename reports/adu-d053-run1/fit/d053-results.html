Adding Oregon to the ADU calibration

Portland and Bend (2016–2025, 20 place-years) were added to the four-state fit. Oregon builds ADUs at several times the rate of any place in Minnesota, North Carolina, Ohio or Texas. Below: what that does to the fitted rate, the backtest, and the spread between states. One finding has to be settled before any config is written.

1 · The fitted rate

Adding Oregon halves θ

θ is the ADU permit rate per 1,000 eligible single-family lots at the reference rent and home value. Each row refits without one state. Rows that include Oregon sit near 0.3; the fit without Oregon is exactly D-048's adopted value (0.659), which confirms the run reproduces it.

3 · Spread between states

A new state's uncertainty widens

τ is the log-SD of the multiplier a state outside the fit gets. With Oregon in, τ̂ rises from 0 to 0.57, so a new state's range widens from roughly ×0.5–×2 to ×0.33–×3.

4 · Portland and Bend by year

Oregon sits far above both fits

Yearly permits against each fit's predictive for a place like it (θ and the place effect drawn). Built counts run 5–25× the median. The fit absorbs this as a large place effect for each city, which is why it can't explain Oregon. The ADU bust after 2018 shows clearly in Portland.

Needs a decision before configs are written

The partial-rule share jumped from 0.3 to 1.0

  • The fit also estimates how much a partial ADU rule (parking required, conditional permit, attached only, small cap) does relative to a full one. Without Oregon, the data couldn't pin it down, so the judgment value 0.3 was used.
  • With Oregon in, it is estimated at 1.0 with a narrow interval. By fit 3's pre-registered rule an estimated value replaces the judgment: partial rules would count as full ones in every scenario.
  • The cause is coding, not behavior. Portland's 2016–2020 rule has blank fields (parking, size cap, owner occupancy). The model fills blanks from pooled odds and puts Portland 86% on "partial". Portland then built hundreds of ADUs a year under what the fit thinks is a partial rule.
  • Portland's rules are well documented (no owner occupancy or parking requirement since 2010, SDC waiver). Filling those fields in config/reforms/or.csv and rerunning would test whether θ and the share move back.
