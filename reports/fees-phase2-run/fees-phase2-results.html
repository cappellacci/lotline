What happens if local governments cut their development fees in half?

About 850 more homes over 2027–2031 in the 50 places we have fees for, on top of about 449,000 that would be built anyway. That is 0.2% more.

Minnesota, North Carolina, Ohio and Texas. Likely range by state is shown below; the method is at the end.

So the story is the difference

Why so few: fees are a sliver of what a home costs to build

For a typical new house in these four states, coded fees are about $7,300, roughly 1.6% of the cost of building it. Halving them saves the builder about $1,800 after landowners take their share through land prices.

Construction, design, interest: 98.4%

Fees: 1.6%. In Oregon, where system development charges are high, fees are about 7% of the cost of a house.

The Handbook assumes fees seven times higher than these places charge

The challenge's benchmark starts from $50,000 a home and cuts $25,000. Real fees here are about $7,300. That is why the benchmark adds about seven times as many homes as a realistic cut.

Raising fees by half would cost about as many homes

The same arithmetic runs in reverse. A rise of half the local fee loses about 860 homes over five years in these places.

Fees charged per unit weigh most on fourplexes

Most places charge water, sewer and impact fees for each dwelling, so a fourplex lot pays about three and a half times a house's fees. Per unit, that makes a cut matter about twice as much for duplexes and fourplexes as for houses. They are a small share of what gets built, so they add few homes in total.

Median fees per lot, MN, NC, OH and TX. Oregon's are four to five times higher: $34,000 for a house and $101,000 for a fourplex.

How this was estimated
  • Fees. Each place's coded fees for a house, a duplex and a fourplex (config/fees/), in 2025 dollars. Missing building-permit and water/sewer charges are filled from the median of coded places (D-065, D-066).
  • Homes without the cut. The baseline forecast for 2027–2031 by building size (the same one every scenario uses).
  • Effect of a cut. Builders keep half the saving (landowners take the rest, range 25–75%). Building rises by 0.3% for each 1% cut in cost (range 0 to 1.75), phased in over about two years. Each building size uses its own fee and its own cost per unit; one Minnesota place with enough parcel data counts lots that newly pencil instead (D-032, D-073).
  • Not counted. Buildings of 5 or more units (no fees are coded for them), effects on prices or rents, approval times, and what governments do without the fee revenue.
  • Ranges. 1,000 runs drawing every uncertain input. The four-state total of 850 is the sum of the state means.
  • Left out. Oregon (the transfer state) and the holdout places. Wilmington, Concord and Gastonia (NC) had no baseline or ACS value.
  • Files. reports/fees-phase2-run/ (run log, totals, summary) and the pencil-out table docs/research/fee-pencil-out.md; method in D-073, branch ws/fees-phase2, PR #356. No outcome was read.
