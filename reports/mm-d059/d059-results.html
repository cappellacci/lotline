The missing-middle backtest with Lubbock set aside

Lubbock's pre-UDC duplex rule was never coded, and the UDC may have tightened duplexes rather than freed them. So Ben had it taken out of the fit and the Texas targets as an unverified rule, and D-054's leave-one-state-out backtest was run again. The main model is the adopted D-055 specification with D-058's zero floor. This is a sensitivity run: Lubbock was dropped after its miss was seen.

Finding 1 · Leave one state out

Without Lubbock, North Carolina and Texas both land inside their ranges

Each state is predicted from a fit to the other three. The dot is what was built; the bar is P10–P90. Texas's four remaining places built 24 against a median of 23. North Carolina's six built 26 against 25. No effect also holds both, so state totals alone can't separate the model from no reform. The outside-evidence rate misses both.

Finding 2 · The fitted rate

The 12-place refit is the adopted value to within 5%

(b′) gives θ 0.130 per 1,000 (0.036–0.88), with the half-way point at 0.5 years. D-055's adopted value is 0.124 (0.033–1.01). Finding 3 · Signal against no effect

The test cities show a reform effect; the fit-set towns are too small to

Held-out log score of (b′) minus no effect, by place. On the 12 fit places the gain is 14.2 (paired SE 9.5), under the 2-SE bar of 19.1. Minneapolis and Durham city, never in any fit, gain 26 and 32 each: both built about twice their own trend. Most fit-set places are small towns that built little or nothing.
