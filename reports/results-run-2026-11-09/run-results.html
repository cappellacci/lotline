Five states, with the baseline the reforms add to

The first real results bundles for MN, NC, OH, OR and TX: the 2027–2031 window (D-061), rules in force by the end of 2027 counted as current (D-064), and, new in this run, the no-policy-change baseline. 1,000 draws per scenario, 4,000 for the baseline. A same-seed sensitivity isolates what D-064 changed.

Finding 1 · The baseline

With no change, each state keeps building near its 2025 pace

Homes permitted a year in the places the baseline forecasts (dots: observed 2019–2025; band: P10–P90 for 2027–2031; line: midpoint). Counts are as reported to Census. The forecast starts at the 2025 level, not the 2021–22 peak, because the regional cycle starts where the state last was. The midpoint drifts down about 2% a year because the cycle is a random walk whose median falls while its mean stays flat.

Finding 3 · What D-064 changed

Counting 2026–27 laws as current trims North Carolina's ADU gain by a third

Same seeds and draws, only the "current rules" date moves: 2025-12-31 (before D-064), 2026-12-31 (D-064's low value) and 2027-12-31 (adopted). North Carolina's ADU midpoint falls from 318 to 201, mostly from S.L. 2026-59 taking effect on 15 January 2027. Missing middle falls 12% in Minnesota and 6% in North Carolina. Texas ADUs fall 4%. Ohio and Oregon do not move: Oregon's 34 missing-middle rules dated 2027 are no stronger than the ones already in force, so D-064's expected fall there did not happen. The 2025-12-31 arm reproduces D-063's published missing-middle numbers exactly.

Finding 4 · Things to check before these numbers go on the page

Oregon's ADU zero and the tier labels need a look

  • Oregon ADUs = 0 in every draw. 118 of 168 places carry a Handbook-equivalent ADU rule (SB 1051 and local codes), and the 50 with no coded rule borrow their prior from those, so they count as already allowed too. SB 1051 skips cities under 2,500 people, so the true figure is probably a little above zero.
  • NC, TX and OR show as "Tier 3: mostly national data". The bundle's tier is the single tier holding the most eligible parcels. In NC, tier 3 holds 1.6 million and tiers 1 and 2 together 1.6 million, so one label calls a half-local state "mostly national". The map and badge read this field.
  • Coverage is high everywhere (96.7% in Ohio to 99.9% in Minnesota of the 2025 units in the permit panel). It is measured against BPS places with a place code; rows without one are outside it.
  • Housing need shares are small: missing middle's midpoint is 0.14% (OR) to 1.1% (OH, shortage) of the need denominator; ADUs 0.04% (NC) to 0.45% (MN).
