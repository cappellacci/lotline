Lotline · exploratory, not adopted · run 2026-11-13 · branch ws/adu-version-c

Rule details fit the past a little better but don't predict new states any better

Version C gives each ADU rule detail its own effect: whether a hearing is needed (not by right), 1 or 2+ extra parking spaces, other restrictions, and city size. It ran against A′, today's model (Form A) refit on the same newly coded rules from PR #417, so the only difference is the structure. The plan was written before the fit (PLAN.md). Neither model predicts a held-out state well enough at any data tier. St. Paul stayed blind.

1 · The rule-detail effects

The effects lean on North Carolina: leave it out and they move to "other restrictions"

Each row is the effect fitted with every state, then with one state left out, as a multiplier on uptake (1 = no effect; the line is ±1 SE). With all five states, needing a hearing cuts uptake to about a quarter, and one parking space cuts it by about 30%. Leaving out North Carolina, where Raleigh's 2021 change sits, removes both parking effects and most of the hearing effect, and "other restrictions" takes their place. The plan said a slope that only one state identifies counts as not identified, and that is what this shows.

2 · A state with its own rules (tier 1)

C raises the medians for NC, TX and OR a little, and overshoots Minnesota more

Each state is predicted by a fit to the other four, using its own coded rules. The bar is P10–P90, the tick is the median and the dot is what was built in 2021–2024. Useful means the dot is inside the bar and within ×2 of the tick. C closes part of the gap in North Carolina (built ÷ median 3.6 → 2.4) and Texas (6.1 → 2.7), but Minnesota goes from 3.6× too high to 5.2× too high. 4 · What the new rule rows did on their own

A′ against the D-079 refit: lower θ, test C now passes, predictions no better

Same model, new rows. θ falls from 0.76 to 0.54 per 1,000 lots (90% range 0.39–0.78), the "other restriction" share rises from 0.4 to 0.7, and the fit set grows from 395 to 491 place-years, because the rows now go back before the old start dates. Test C passes in all five states (lowest ratios 0.99–1.00, within D-082's 1%), and the Franklin County roll check now passes. The tier medians are no closer: North Carolina's tier-1 built ÷ median moves from 2.7 to 3.6.

What this says, and the choices it leaves

  • Read: rule details are not why the model misses whole states. C fits the past slightly better, but it predicts held-out places no better, and its effects come mostly from North Carolina.
  • The gap is still at the state and city level: Minnesota builds far less per lot than any rule detail explains, and the active cities in NC, OR and TX build 3 to 5 times the median.
  • Open 1: whether to accept PR #417's rows anyway. They help on their own: test C now passes everywhere, and the 10 events let D-079's rule-change term be fitted.
  • Open 2: whether to keep version C as a candidate for a dated method entry, or drop it. My pick is to drop it as a predictor and keep the code on the branch.
