Cities that choose the reform build fast; it doesn't forecast better

After St. Paul built faster than C4 predicted, we tested a separate ramp for cities that adopt missing middle themselves and for places a state law requires to. In the fit the split is clear: local adopters reach half speed in about half a year, places under Oregon's HB 2001 in four. But scored on states the fit didn't see, neither a refit with St. Paul nor the split ramp beats C4 by the pre-registered two standard errors. C4 stays, and the scenario's ramp, for a state law, is the same either way.

Every leave-one-state-out fold with both groups finds the same split. In five-year rates: 0.79 new 2–4 unit buildings per 1,000 lots a year for local adopters against 0.22 for state-required places (C4: 0.24).

The local ramp rests on 37 places with 64 post-reform years, most of them small Oregon towns with one or two years of data. Adding St. Paul to a single shared ramp makes held-out scores worse: one fast city pulls the ramp and the rate for the slow Oregon majority. R2's θ interval (0.38–2.09 per 1,000) is narrower than C4's because it holds the pair of ramps fixed.

For Ben

What changes, and what doesn't

Config stays at C4. For the Handbook scenario, a state law, the state-required ramp (4 years) is the same as C4's, so the statewide numbers don't move. What's new is an explanation for St. Paul's miss: cities that choose the reform build within a year. That fits as a stated finding and a "fast ramp" sensitivity for places that adopt on their own, if you want it shown.
