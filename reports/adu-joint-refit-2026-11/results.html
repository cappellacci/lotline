The five-state ADU refit: Form A stays, and no data tier earns a claim

D-079 pre-registered a contest between the current ADU model (Form A, D-048) and a regression challenger (Form B). Both were fitted to 64 places in five states and asked to predict 2021–2024 ADU permits in a state they had not seen, first with no local data (tier 0), then with the state's own coded local rules (tier 1), then with its largest city's counts (tier 2). Neither form predicts well enough in 4 of the 5 states at any tier, so by the rule written before the run Form A is kept and the tool shows tier-2 ranges. St. Paul stayed blind throughout.

1 · A state with no ADU data (tier 0)

With no local data, Form A's range usually holds the truth but its middle is off 3× to 15×

Each state is predicted by a fit to the other four, with local rules borrowed by city size from those four states and lots from the ACS. The bar is P10–P90, the tick is the median and the dot is what was built in 2021–2024 (each state's largest city left out). A prediction counts as useful if the dot is inside the bar and within ×2 of the tick. Form A overshoots Minnesota and undershoots the other three. Form B is useful in Minnesota and North Carolina only because its bars are very wide (NC: 17 to 5,374). It misses Oregon by a factor of 26.

2 · More data, tiers 1 and 2

Adding a state's own rules or its biggest city barely moves the median

Useful states per tier, against the bar of 4. Ohio's one fit place is its tier-2 city, so it has nothing left to score, and the bar needs all four other states. Form A never scores a state. Form B's tier-2 update collapses onto 1–3 of its 20,000 draws in MN, NC and OR, so its tier-2 ranges can't be trusted. The same pattern holds when the field undercount is set to its 5th or 95th percentile and when Asheville's low-recall years are dropped (Form B then reaches 3 states at tier 1).

3 · Test A · places new since D-053

New places: Form A passes only in Texas

Each new place is predicted with a fresh place effect. A state passes if at least 60% of its new places are inside P10–P90 and the median observed ÷ predicted is within ×2. North Carolina's seven new cities mostly sit inside their ranges, but they built about three times the median.

4 · Tests B and C

The model expects more suburban ADUs than the parcel rolls show, and the no-reform floor sits below today's counts

Test B: the Wake County towns gained 5 ADUs on the rolls in 2020–2023, and Form A's range was 28–218 (Form B's 14–77). Three Franklin County towns gained 1, and the ranges were 2–102 and 2–29. Both forms fail in both states.

Test C (Form A only) asks whether the engine's no-reform median is at least what places already build. It fails in all five states: 43 of 73 places fall short. Most of them build under one ADU a year, but Durham, Asheville, Wilmington, San Antonio, Fort Worth, Medford, Salem and Columbus also fall 2–20% short.

each dot = one place · ratio = no-reform median ÷ counted ADUs a year · pass needs every dot at or right of 1
5 · The refit rate

Minnesota holds θ down; without it θ doubles

θ is Form A's ADU permit rate per 1,000 eligible lots at reference rent and value, with a 90% profile interval. Leaving out NC, OH, TX or OR moves it by under 20%. Leaving out Minnesota doubles it, which matches the tier-0 pattern: Minnesota builds far fewer ADUs per lot than the other states. D-053's 0.31 used a different fit set and m_x = 1.0, so it is not a like-for-like comparison.

  • Open 2: how to respond to the failures. Each fix would be a new dated entry and a new run, because nothing may be tuned after scoring. The failures point at a state-level gap (Minnesota low, the others high) and at a no-reform floor that sits below current counts.
