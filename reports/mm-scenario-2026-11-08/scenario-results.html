Five states under the Handbook missing-middle rule

This is the first run of the new scenario module. Every single-family-zoned lot may hold 2–6 homes by right, compared with each place's rules at the end of 2025. Rates come from the fitted model (D-055 values), with 4,000 draws per state. These are added homes over five years, net of the houses torn down to make room. No permits were read.

Finding 1 · The headline

Texas adds about 6,000 homes in five years; the other states between 100 and 1,500

Median net added homes over five years, with the P10–P90 bar. The mean (◆) sits about twice the median, because a few draws have a high statewide rate. Texas leads on sheer lot count: 8.1 million eligible parcels, nearly all without a missing-middle rule today. Oregon adds least, because HB 2001 already allows most of what the Handbook would in its 96 coded cities.

Finding 2 · The range

The range spans a factor of about 19. Most of that is one uncertain statewide rate.

Every place in a draw shares the same θ, and θ's 90% interval spans a factor of 31 (0.033–1.01 per 1,000). Place-to-place spread (α ≈ 8.4) mostly averages out across hundreds of places, so the statewide P90 is about 4.5× the median. These are wide ranges by design. The fit set is 13 places, and transfer passed while the signal over no effect stayed under 2 SE (D-059).

Finding 3 · From buildings to homes

Teardowns take back about a third of the new homes

Median buildings, the homes in them, the houses they replace, and the net. Most eligible lots hold a house (82–98%), so most new 2–4 unit buildings replace one. Each building averages 2.7 homes here, because the draw's range runs up to Minnesota's 4.0 (on 10 buildings). Holding it at the pooled 2.08 cuts net homes by about 30% (Finding 1, orange).

Finding 4 · Where and when

Big cities and growing suburban counties lead, and year 1 is half of year 5

Share of each state's mean added homes in its top five places. Unincorporated suburban counties (Harris, Montgomery, Wake, Hamilton) rank next to the big cities because they hold huge numbers of eligible lots and have no coded rule. The top five carry 18–25% in these states. Over the five years, yearly medians ramp from about 50% of the mature rate in year 1 to the full rate by year 4. That is the fast ramp D-055 fitted.
